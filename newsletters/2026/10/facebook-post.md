The September family history newsletter is now available.

This month I have been looking at wills, testaments, inventories, confirmations and administrations — not as a legal-history exercise, but from the family historian’s point of view.

The records take us from Patrick Cairncross’s 1737 Scottish testament and Henry Hinshelwood’s silver tea service, through Archibald Whitelaw’s family stretching from Scotland to London, Lawrence Stephens’s £5,000 administration, William Reynolds’s family and charity bequests, and William Loch Orton’s estate after his death in Calcutta.

These records can reveal far more than who inherited what. They can reconnect married daughters with their birth families, give occupations and addresses, show debts and possessions, trace migration, and sometimes preserve wonderfully ordinary details of everyday life.
